## P1 — High

### [R-007] Incident commit and notification intent are not atomic

- **Type:** Review finding.
- **Priority rationale:** A detected change can permanently miss its alert after a crash or database error. History remains available, so P1 rather than P0.
- **Location:** `internal/app/app.go:249-278`; `internal/store/store.go:778-785`, `internal/store/store.go:812-824`; `internal/notify/notify.go:412-424`; `internal/engine/engine.go:332-338`.
- **Evidence:** The engine commits runtime state and events before Notifier.Queue separately inserts destination deliveries. Inserts are separate per destination. The daemon retries existing outbox rows, with no durable fan-out checkpoint/replay. A later unchanged incident only updates its timestamp and does not reopen the event.
- **Impact:** A crash between commit and enqueue, queue write error, or expiry of the shared persistence context leaves a recorded incident with missing deliveries. Partial fan-out can alert only some destinations; identical later scans do not repair it.
- **Recommendation:** Persist notification intent with the event/state transaction. Either insert outbox rows in that transaction or mark committed events for durable fan-out with an idempotent event/destination key. Cover baseline/lifecycle events that produce alerts as well.
- **Acceptance / validation:** Inject failure after state persistence and halfway through fan-out, restart, and verify every intended destination gets one queued delivery without needing another scan.
- **Effort:** M.
- **Confidence:** High; boundaries and missing replay observed, crash outcome inferred.
- **Trade-off / challenge:** Durable intent supports at-least-once delivery, not exactly-once provider receipt. Decide destination membership at event time so replay does not unexpectedly alert newly added destinations.

### [R-008] New alerts disappear during managed-key outages

- **Type:** Review finding.
- **Priority rationale:** Temporary key unavailability silently loses new alerts for destinations that remain enabled.
- **Location:** `internal/notify/notify.go:125-158`, `internal/notify/notify.go:397-424`, `internal/notify/notify.go:452-457`; `internal/notify/notify_test.go:298-309`.
- **Evidence:** Reload marks destinations locked for missing, unsafe, or invalid keys. destinationSnapshot excludes locked records, and Queue uses that snapshot to select destinations. Queue succeeds without those rows. Drain defers existing locked deliveries but cannot recover deliveries never created. The outage test checks deployment enqueue without requiring a retained managed delivery.
- **Impact:** Restoring the key after an incident opens does not recover its alert. Scans/history continue, making the omission easy to miss.
- **Recommendation:** Separate enqueue eligibility from decrypted sendability. Queue enabled managed IDs/revisions even when locked, without requiring plaintext URLs. Let Drain defer until key recovery; keep paused/deleted destinations excluded.
- **Acceptance / validation:** Remove the key, queue a new event, and assert a managed outbox row with zero attempts. Restore the original key and verify delivery. Cover wrong key, unsafe permissions, and explicitly paused destinations.
- **Effort:** S.
- **Confidence:** High; deterministic source branch behavior.
- **Trade-off / challenge:** Long outages create backlog. Define its age/expiry explicitly and expose it instead of silently discarding events.

### [R-009] Delivery blocks heartbeat and schedule reconciliation

- **Type:** Review finding.
- **Priority rationale:** Provider slowness can make a working scan engine appear dead and delay schedule changes; this affects availability and daemon coordination.
- **Location:** `internal/app/app.go:318-355`, `internal/app/app.go:282`; `internal/notify/notify.go:427-479`; `internal/store/store.go:1362-1401`.
- **Evidence:** Startup/periodic Drain runs synchronously in the loop serving the 30-second heartbeat and schedule wakeups. Drain claims 100 rows, sends serially, and holds drainMu. Each send has a 15-second timeout; mutex waiting and sends do not accept caller cancellation. Health and lease takeover use a two-minute stale threshold. Periodic draining can also wait behind scan-triggered draining.
- **Impact:** Nine sequential 15-second timeouts exceed the stale threshold; a full batch can occupy roughly 25 minutes. Health fails, schedule reconciliation waits, and another daemon can acquire the stale lease. Shutdown also waits for delivery work. Already registered cron callbacks can continue; this does not imply all scans immediately stop.
- **Recommendation:** Use one application-owned delivery worker independent of heartbeat/reconciliation, with bounded passes and cancellation checks before sends. Wake it after enqueue instead of draining synchronously from scan completion. Retain durable claims for cross-process coordination.
- **Acceptance / validation:** Inject a blocking sender/clock and backlog; verify heartbeats remain fresh, schedule pause/update takes effect promptly, and shutdown meets a documented bound. A competing daemon must not acquire the healthy daemon's lease.
- **Effort:** M.
- **Confidence:** High; blocking structure and timeout arithmetic observed, provider outage not executed.
- **Trade-off / challenge:** A cancellation wrapper must not leave unlimited sends running in background. If the provider cannot be cancelled, cap in-flight sends and document shutdown latency.

### [R-010] Authentication bootstrap discards deep links

- **Type:** Review finding.
- **Priority rationale:** Refreshing/bookmarking a protected page loses navigation context; data remains safe and in-app navigation works.
- **Location:** `src/main.tsx:60-66`, `src/main.tsx:42`.
- **Evidence:** App renders AuthRoutes while setup/session queries are unresolved. Its wildcard immediately navigates to /setup when configured status is undefined, or /login before session resolution. Once authenticated, Shell redirects these paths to /. ProtectedApp's loading guard occurs too late to preserve the original path.
- **Impact:** A logged-in user loading /jobs/<id> or /incidents can land on Overview, with misleading setup/login flashes on slow responses.
- **Recommendation:** Resolve pending/configured/authenticated/error state before redirecting. Preserve the requested internal path through login. Treat server errors as retryable errors instead of unconfigured state.
- **Acceptance / validation:** Delay setup/session responses independently; load and reload protected deep links and assert the URL/page remains intact. Cover logged-out return-to, initial setup, and setup-status failure.
- **Effort:** S.
- **Confidence:** High; redirect sequence observed in source, not newly executed in-browser.
- **Trade-off / challenge:** A short loading state is preferable to guessing authentication status; reject external return-to destinations.

### [R-011] Retention deletes definitions referenced by retained scans

- **Type:** Review finding.
- **Priority rationale:** Routine maintenance irreversibly reduces historical provenance, although observations remain readable.
- **Location:** `internal/store/store.go:1313-1315`, `internal/store/store.go:1339-1344`; `internal/model/model.go:27-51`; `internal/app/app.go:206`.
- **Evidence:** Pruning protects active baseline scans but deletes old non-current revisions solely by revision creation time. The comment says scans retain snapshots; those snapshots contain observations and partial scope, not the complete job definition such as TCP mode, assume-alive, expansion limit, timing, and timeout. Scan stores a revision number/hash, not those settings.
- **Impact:** An old revision can produce a recent scan, then be replaced and immediately pruned. A protected baseline can outlive its revision indefinitely. Original settings cannot be reconstructed from the hash.
- **Recommendation:** Preserve revisions referenced by any retained scan, including baselines, with a dependency guard. Prune scans before newly unreferenced revisions. Alternatively persist complete job definitions on scans at additional storage cost.
- **Acceptance / validation:** Create an old revision with a recent scan and a protected baseline, supersede it, prune, and verify referenced definitions survive. Once the last referencing scan is eligible and removed, its superseded revision should be pruned.
- **Effort:** S.
- **Confidence:** High; model and deletion predicates directly establish the gap.
- **Trade-off / challenge:** Keeping one small definition per referenced revision costs less than duplicating it for every scan.
